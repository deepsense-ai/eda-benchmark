### Key Discoveries & Data Cleaning Pipeline
During the research phase, I conducted an exhaustive audit of the dataset and identified several critical issues which were resolved via a standard database/ETL integrity model:
1. **Entity Integrity (Deduplication):** Found and removed **96 duplicate rows** representing redundant data entry (identical employee, date, hours, tasks, and facility).
2. **Referential Integrity (Employees & Tasks):** Excluded **135 rows** belonging to unrecognized employee IDs, and **88 rows** with task IDs not listed in `tasks.csv` (where their compensation is unknown).
3. **Domain Constraints (Dates):** Excluded **22 records** with physically impossible dates (`2025-04-31` and `2025-06-31`).
4. **Semantic Consistency:** Excluded **14 rows** where hours worked was `0` but `salary_amount` was positive and `bonus` was negative.
5. **Syntactic Robustness (Malformed Lists):** Successfully recovered **20 malformed task lists** (e.g. missing opening or closing brackets) using regular expressions to extract valid digit arrays.

### Billing Periods & Logic
The earliest record in the dataset is **Tuesday, March 4, 2025**. Accordingly, the 4 billing periods start on the first Tuesday of each month and end on the day preceding the next period:
* **March 2025 Period:** `2025-03-04` to `2025-03-31` (inclusive)
* **April 2025 Period:** `2025-04-01` to `2025-05-05` (inclusive)
* **May 2025 Period:** `2025-05-06` to `2025-06-02` (inclusive)
* **June 2025 Period:** `2025-06-03` to `2025-06-30` (inclusive)

The final salaries are computed as `(hours_worked * hourly_rate) + task_bonuses` for each period and rounded to the nearest integer using standard mathematical rounding (round half up).
